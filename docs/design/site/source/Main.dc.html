What Learn Quiz records, and what it never does.

Recorded
Answers, how sure you were, how long each took, and progress toward the benchmark.
Who sees it
You, and your company’s Learn Quiz admins, such as your manager or training lead.
What it’s for
Training and development: what to review next, and when you’ve reached the role’s benchmark.

Never

  • No camera, sensors or health data.
  • No personality scores.
  • No ranking against coworkers.

No biometrics, no hidden tests. A person decides.

Built on how memory works.

  • Trying to recall beats rereading.

    Pulling an answer from memory makes it last longer than reading the page again.

    Roediger & Karpicke, 2006, Psychological Science · Dunlosky et al., 2013, Psychological Science in the Public Interest

  • Spaced reviews make knowledge last.

    Reviews spread over days hold knowledge longer than one long session. FSRS, an open-source scheduler, picks the day for each review.

    Cepeda, Pashler, Vul, Wixted & Rohrer, 2006, Psychological Bulletin

  • Knowing why makes practice count.

    Feedback that explains the right answer corrects mistakes and carries over to new situations.

    Butler, Karpicke & Roediger, 2008, JEP: Learning, Memory, and Cognition · Butler, Godbole & Marsh, 2013, Journal of Educational Psychology

  • Confidence shows what is shaky.

    Asking how sure you are separates lucky guesses from real knowledge. Mistakes made with confidence are the ones feedback corrects best.

    Butterfield & Metcalfe, 2001, JEP: Learning, Memory, and Cognition · Metcalfe, 2017, Annual Review of Psychology
